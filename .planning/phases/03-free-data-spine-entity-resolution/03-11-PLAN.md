<objective>
Ship survivorship, merge and unmerge (D-14, D-19, D-20, DEDUP-02), including the three
`SECURITY DEFINER` functions that are the only write path to `business_merges` and
`merge_candidates`.

Purpose: **merges are rows, never deletes** (ARCHITECTURE anti-pattern 9). The loser gets
`status='merged'` and `merged_into_id`; nothing is removed.

🔴 **`source_records.business_id` is NEVER re-pointed on merge.** Each source record keeps
pointing at the business it created; only the winner's `*_source_id` provenance pairs move.
Three consequences, all desirable: "every parent record survives the merge" (criterion 4)
becomes **structural** rather than a procedure somebody can forget; unmerge restores the loser's
fields by re-reading its own source records, with no snapshot needed; and the winner — whose
pairs *were* overwritten — is the only side that needs `business_merges.winner_fields_before`.

🔴 **One survivorship function, two call sites, never two implementations.** The merge and the
unmerge both call `survive()`.

🔴 **Pairwise merges must be cluster-aware.** Measured: `"RIO STONE PRODUCTS, INC."` matches
**both** `"RIO Stone Products"` and `"Rio Stone Products Inc"` at similarity 1.000. Process
candidates in descending score and resolve both sides through `coalesce(merged_into_id, id)`
before each write — without that re-point, the second merge writes a `merged_into_id` pointing
at an already-merged loser and the chain breaks. That is mutation **M25**.

Output: two resolve modules, one `db:custom` migration with three definers, two DB test files.
</objective>
