# Project Research Summary

**Project:** Siteless
**Domain:** Multi-source local-business discovery + web-presence verification SaaS ("no-website" lead generation), scheduled ingestion under a hard budget cap + mobile-first phone triage — Rio Grande Valley → Texas
**Researched:** 2026-09-20 (six parallel research files: STACK, FEATURES, ARCHITECTURE, PITFALLS, COMPETITORS, DATA-SOURCES)
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH overall — HIGH on platform pricing, Google/Yelp/Meta ToS text, and package versions (all read live from official sources on 2026-09-20); MEDIUM on entity-resolution heuristics, verification-rule tuning, and RGV-specific coverage numbers, which converge strongly across sources but still need empirical measurement in Phase 2.

## Executive Summary

Siteless is a scheduled, multi-source local-business discovery pipeline with a verification layer as its actual product and a mobile-first triage PWA as its daily interface. Six independent researchers converged on the same reordering of the brief: **Google Maps Platform's Terms of Service forbid building a listings database from Places content** — only `place_id` (indefinite) and lat/lng (30 days) may be cached, and §3.2.3 explicitly names "business listings database" and "listings or directory service" as prohibited uses. This flips PROJECT.md's four-layered-source design. **Overture Maps (57,494 RGV places, CDLA-Permissive-2.0, free, commercially storable) and TX Comptroller active sales-tax permits (34,928 RGV outlets, public domain, reaches sole proprietors) become the primary, durable enumerators; Google Places becomes a transient, authoritative verifier** whose response is read for a `websiteUri` boolean and mostly discarded. This is not a compliance footnote — it is the schema (a provenance foreign key that only lets durable fields cite durable sources), the build order (free-data spine before the first Places call), and the reason the design survives a "no" from danlo's lawyer with a config change instead of a rewrite.

The second load-bearing finding is economic: `websiteUri`, `nationalPhoneNumber`, `rating`, and `userRatingCount` — every field the product depends on — are Enterprise-SKU fields on Places API (New), with only 1,000 free calls/month. Text Search bills per *request* (up to 20 places), not per result, so enumeration itself is cheap (~$1.75 per 1,000 businesses, largely inside the free tier) — but PROJECT.md's literal "nightly full crawl" requirement is arithmetically impossible under $50/month (a naive nightly full sweep of the four-cluster RGV grid prices out around $91–261/month across independent models). The architecture that fits the cap inverts the schedule instead: **free, unlimited Text Search Essentials (IDs Only) runs nightly for change detection; the expensive Enterprise field mask runs weekly or monthly over a shortlist; Firecrawl supplies the receipts layer.** Five of the six research files converge on a steady-state estimate in the **$21–37/month** range (they disagree on the exact number — see Gaps — because they model tile counts, overlap, and Firecrawl's per-search credit cost differently), leaving 25–58% headroom. **Vercel Pro (~$20/month) is a separate, effectively mandatory infrastructure cost** — Hobby cron fires once a day with ±59 minutes of jitter — that must be surfaced to danlo as a decision distinct from the $50 data cap.

Competitively, this space is crowded but shallow: 20+ products already ship a "no website" filter as their entire pitch, and the field is thin on scoring, near-empty on verification evidence, and **empty on measured accuracy** — not one of 23 surveyed products publishes a false-positive rate, and only one (Webleadr, a PWA at $0.24–0.28/verified lead) is mobile-first or attempts real verification. Siteless's stated differentiators — receipts, a measured FP rate, cost-per-verified-lead, and phone-first triage — are exactly the empty columns in that matrix, and three of them are nearly free once the cost ledger and the append-only evidence table exist from day one, which is why they must be Phase 0/1 infrastructure rather than a later feature. The architectural risk is concentrated at the two ends: **Phase 0's schema carries nine of the seventeen catalogued pitfalls** (candidate-vs-lead state separation, a sixth `unverifiable` verdict, two-name-field provenance, `place_id`-vs-lead-UUID separation, `org_id NOT NULL`, the tile grid, the budget ledger, suppressions, `timestamptz`+`APP_TZ`) — cheap now, a cross-cutting migration later — and **verification quality is where a naive build silently fails** (RGV bilingual entity resolution, `*.business.site` as a guaranteed-dead-but-high-value lead, service-area trades businesses excluded by a default flag, and Yelp/Meta being legally and economically incompatible with v1).

## Cross-Cutting Findings (converged across all six research files)

These eight findings were reached independently by multiple researchers. Each is stated once here; the roadmapper should treat every one as load-bearing, not optional.

1. **Google Maps Platform ToS inverts the source hierarchy.** Only `place_id` (indefinite) and lat/lng (30 days) may be cached from Places content; §3.2.3(d)(iii) forbids a "listings or directory service." Places becomes a **transient verifier** (stores `place_id` + a derived boolean, discards the rest); **Overture + TX Comptroller become the primary, durable enumerators**. The §3.2.3(d)(iii) question ("is a lead-qualification tool a listings/directory service?") is a genuine grey area needing danlo's own legal call before the first production Places call — build the ingestion behind an interface so "no" is a config change. **Lands in:** Phase 0 (schema — provenance FK constraint, ephemeral-vs-durable table split) and Phase 2/3 (free-data spine before the Places adapter).
2. **Places economics force a rotating/weekly schedule, not a literal nightly full crawl.** `websiteUri`/`nationalPhoneNumber`/`rating`/`userRatingCount` are Enterprise-SKU fields (1,000 free calls/mo); Text Search bills ~$1.75/1,000 businesses (20/request) vs. Place Details at ~11x worse per business — **never enumerate with Place Details**. Text Search Essentials (IDs Only) is free and unlimited and should drive nightly change detection; Enterprise sweeps run weekly/monthly over a shortlist. Steady state converges on **~$21–37/month** (files disagree on the exact figure — see Gaps). The field mask is a hard-coded allow-list, and the reserve→settle budget ledger is Phase 1 infrastructure, built before the first billable call. **Lands in:** Phase 1 (budget ledger, field-mask SKU classifier) and Phase 3 (Places adapter schedule design) — and it should trigger a PROJECT.md amendment to the "nightly by default" requirement.
3. **Yelp, Meta Graph API, Apify, and SerpApi are all out of v1 — for different specific reasons, not a blanket "budget" reason.** Yelp Fusion: $229/month floor, 24-hour cache ceiling, no-database and no-rating-blending clauses, and it has no website field at all. Meta Pages Search: no consent-free discovery path exists (removed 2020; Page Public Content Access needs App Review you can't schedule). Apify/SerpApi: both derive from Google, so using them to "verify" a Places result is the same source laundered, not independent evidence, and each also carries its own ToS exposure with no cost advantage. Detect Yelp/Facebook/Instagram presence from **web-search-derived URLs (Firecrawl) and Overture's `socials[]` array** (43,979 free, storable Facebook URLs already in RGV Overture data) instead. **Lands in:** this is a pre-roadmap decision (amend PROJECT.md's data-source list before phases are planned) with implementation landing in Phase 4 (Verification) and Phase 5 (Scoring — score only on Google's rating/count to avoid the cross-platform blending prohibition).
4. **The verdict taxonomy needs a sixth class, and dead builder-URLs are a prime lead signature.** Add `unverifiable`/`blocked` alongside the five PROJECT.md classes — bot walls, login-gated social pages, and timeouts must never collapse into `no_presence`, or every bot wall becomes a false positive; it must be non-promotable to the triage queue by policy. Separately, **`*.business.site` URLs are guaranteed dead since 2024-06-10** (Google killed GBP-built sites 2024-03-05) — any `websiteUri` still pointing there is a business that demonstrably wanted a site and lost it, detectable by a 3-line string match with no network call, and is one of the highest-value, easiest-to-pitch leads in the dataset. Receipts are immutable and stored separately from a versioned, pure classifier function, so re-classification after a rule change costs **$0 — a full replay over stored evidence**, never a re-crawl. **Lands in:** the enum and the evidence/verdict schema split are Phase 0 (retrofitting a Postgres enum value later is a cross-cutting migration); the rule logic and the `business.site` hard rule are Phase 4/5.
5. **Vercel Pro (~$20/month) is effectively required, separate from the $50 data cap.** Hobby cron fires once per day with ±59 minutes of jitter and caps functions at 300s; Pro gives sub-hour cron accuracy, 800s functions, and 7-day Workflow retention. Surface this to danlo explicitly as an infrastructure decision distinct from the data budget — the two caps should never be confused in the dashboard or the roadmap's cost accounting. **Lands in:** Phase 0/1 (infra setup) and Phase 6 (scheduler design, which depends on Pro-tier cron accuracy).
6. **Live-measured RGV universe reframes "coverage."** Overture: 57,494 TX-side RGV places (19,414 no website — 33.8%; 16,882 social-only-no-website — 29.4%). TX Comptroller active sales-tax permits: 34,928 RGV outlets (9,896 across the four target industry clusters by one count; ~23,978 by another NAICS-range count — see Gaps), 38% sole proprietors (answers PROJECT.md's "does the enumeration reach sole proprietors" question — yes, via sales tax, not franchise tax). OSM: 2,674 RGV features total — **skip, 4.6% of Overture's coverage and dominated by exactly the wrong businesses (fast food, fuel, banks)**. A naive RGV lat/lng bounding box is **42% Mexico** (Reynosa + Matamoros are the two largest "RGV cities" in an unfiltered box) — every geography preset, including "radius around a point," must filter on `region='TX'`/`country='US'` or clip to county polygons. Overture's `categories` field is **removed in the `2026-09-23.0` release** (three days after this research) — build against `basic_category` from day one; it's already 97.8% populated. **Lands in:** Phase 2 (free-data spine ingestion) and Phase 0 (geography schema must support county/country clipping, not just a bounding box).
7. **Competitively, the "no website" filter is table stakes; verification, receipts, and measured accuracy are the entire opportunity.** Webleadr is the only mobile-first/PWA competitor and the only one attempting verification, at $0.24–0.28/lead — worth buying this week as competitive intelligence. Zero of 23 surveyed products measure or display a false-positive rate; zero show a "what was searched, what was found, when" evidence trail. One Apify actor with the best-in-market classification taxonomy has 1.88 stars from 993 users despite 100%-successful runs — the strongest available evidence that naive no-website detection, delivered exactly as advertised, still fails users because it isn't verified. **Lands in:** validates the whole roadmap's emphasis on Phase 4 (receipts) and Phase 8 (measured FP rate) as the product's actual moat, not a nice-to-have.
8. **Build order consensus (all sources agree on the dependency graph, not just a preference):** Foundations → {Budget ledger and Free-data spine, in parallel} → Places verifier (transient) → Verification + receipts → Classifier + scorer (pure, versioned) → **a hand-triggered vertical slice on one city × one industry, eyeballing ~50 verdicts, before the scheduler exists** → {Scheduler, Triage PWA, CRM outbox, in parallel once the pipeline is trustworthy} → false-positive feedback loop. The manual-slice gate exists because a nightly crawl that produces confident nonsense is worse than no crawl. **Lands in:** this is the shape of "Implications for Roadmap" below, applied directly.

## Key Findings

### Recommended Stack

Next.js 16.3.5 (App Router, Turbopack default) + React 19.3.0 + TypeScript **6.0.3, not 7.x** (typescript-eslint's peer range hard-blocks TS 7 until a compatible major ships) on Vercel Pro, with a new, standalone Supabase Postgres project (never BIS's shared `tlbkbmlrfafquucsmsmm`) accessed through Drizzle ORM (schema, types, and `pgPolicy`-based RLS in one file) over the transaction-mode pooler with `prepare: false`. Auth is Clerk mirroring BIS's pattern, but note the Clerk-to-Supabase JWT template was deprecated 2025-04-01 — use Supabase's native third-party auth, and Clerk's session-token v2 nests org claims under `o`, not the flat `org_id` BIS's older helper reads; copying BIS's RLS helper verbatim yields silent zero-row policies. Orchestration is Vercel Workflow DevKit (`workflow@4.8.9`) for durable, resumable pipeline runs, paired with Postgres as the actual work queue (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`) so the budget reservation, job claim, and receipt write share one transaction — a managed broker (Vercel Queues, still in beta) can not offer that. PWA layer is Serwist (the Turbopack-compatible successor to the now-dead `next-pwa`) plus `idb` for an offline triage outbox plus `motion` for swipe gestures plus `@tanstack/react-query` for offline-tolerant mutation queuing.

**Core technologies:**
- Next.js 16.3.5 + React 19.3.0 — app framework, PWA shell; note `middleware.ts` to `app/proxy.ts`, and Clerk session checks belong in layouts/route handlers, not the proxy (CVE-2025-29927 context)
- Drizzle ORM 0.45.2 + Supabase Postgres — typed schema + migrations + RLS policies in one place; ships pg_trgm/fuzzystrmatch/unaccent/PostGIS, which is the entire dedupe engine
- Vercel Workflow DevKit + Postgres work queue — durable per-step retries and observability for the ingestion pipeline; budget reservation and job claim share a transaction
- Clerk (native Supabase third-party auth, v2 session claims) — org-scoped auth mirroring BIS, with the deprecated-JWT-template trap called out explicitly
- Firecrawl (`firecrawl@4.41.0`) — the web-search verification layer; own app key required (the Claude MCP connector does not transfer)
- Serwist + idb + motion + react-query — installable, offline-tolerant, swipe-gesture PWA triage

### Expected Features

The market has fully commoditized "filter: no website" — 20+ tools ship it as their whole pitch. Siteless's core value sits one step past the market: a verdict with receipts and a measured false-positive rate, which nobody in this category publishes.

**Must have (table stakes):** industry x geography search presets (RGV built-in, Texas-wide as a bigger preset); candidate list with sort/filter; lead card with call-ready facts; five/six-way verdict (never a boolean); dedupe across sources into one lead; accept/reject-with-reason/snooze triage with undo; tap-to-call; CSV export; CRM handoff on accept; scheduled re-runs with "new since last run"; cost visibility against the cap.

**Should have (differentiators):** per-lead receipts one tap from the card (the trust mechanism — nobody in the category has this); measured, displayed false-positive rate (zero competitors publish one); dead-`business.site`/parked detection as a named high-value class; social-only/directory-only as the top of the ranking, not a checkbox; glass-box score with a visible, persisted breakdown; hard budget cap enforced by the scheduler with cost-per-verified-lead on the dashboard; phone-first offline-tolerant swipe triage (held by exactly one competitor); change detection that auto-retires a lead once it gets a website.

**Defer (v1.x / v2+):** editable score weights with a re-score preview; free-source cross-reference beyond the Phase 2 spine (deeper Comptroller/OSM/DBA reconciliation); a generic outbound webhook for non-BIS buyers; likely-mobile-number flagging; AI-drafted outreach and mock-site previews (explicitly out of scope — TCPA/CAN-SPAM exposure with zero consent infrastructure); native per-CRM OAuth integrations; ML/predictive scoring (no labelled outcomes exist yet); multi-tenant billing (schema is org-scoped from day one, but billing waits for org #2).

### Architecture Approach

The system is built so the legal structure and the good-engineering structure are the same structure: Google is a detector, not a store (Places responses land in a TTL'd cache keyed to `place_id`; the canonical business record is sourced from Overture/Comptroller/the business's own site); evidence and verdict are separated (verification workers write immutable receipts and never decide a classification; a pure, versioned function reads receipts and emits a verdict, making re-classification a free replay); and nothing spends money outside a single reserve-then-call-then-settle budget governor (a race-free conditional `UPDATE` on one row per org per month, verified against PostgreSQL READ COMMITTED re-evaluation semantics — no read-then-write, no advisory lock).

**Major components:**
1. **Source adapters** — one per external source (Google Places, Overture, TX Comptroller, OSM-skip, Firecrawl), each implementing a uniform interface with `estimateCost()` mandatory in the type signature so nothing can spend outside the meter
2. **Entity resolution** — normalize, then block (never compare all pairs), then weighted score, then three-tier merge (auto/review/distinct), reversible, never a delete
3. **Verification workers** — a cheapest-first probe ladder (free Overture/DNS/HTTP signals first, Firecrawl search/scrape last), writing only receipts, never a verdict
4. **Classifier + scorer** — pure functions, no I/O, versioned, reading only from stored receipts; this purity is what makes the false-positive feedback loop cost $0 to iterate
5. **Budget governor** — atomic reserve/settle against a monthly cap, independently backstopped by a per-API daily quota cap in the Google Cloud console (a code bug in the ledger still cannot produce a surprise invoice)
6. **Scheduler + run orchestrator** — Vercel Cron (idempotent tick, UTC-safe hour) triggers a Vercel Workflow per due search, tiled by cluster x city to stay under per-run step/event limits
7. **Triage PWA + CRM outbox** — offline-tolerant, idempotent-by-client-UUID mutations; a transactional outbox delivers accepted leads to BIS with an idempotency key, never an inline HTTP call from the accept transaction

### Critical Pitfalls

1. **The field mask silently selects the billing tier, and every field Siteless needs is Enterprise.** One stray field (e.g. adding `reviews`) promotes an entire sweep to the most expensive SKU with a green test suite. Avoid: one typed field-mask constant, a `skuForFieldMask()` pure function unit-tested per field against Google's own tier table, `*` unreachable from production at the type level.
2. **A literal nightly full crawl does not fit $50/month, and the failure surfaces on day 31, not day 1** (works fine inside the first week's free-tier headroom, then the cap trips and leads stop flowing). Avoid: rotating partitions / weekly-or-monthly Enterprise sweeps plus free nightly IDs-only change detection; model the cost against the real cell list before writing the scheduler, as a committed test, not a spreadsheet.
3. **`websiteUri` absent does not mean no website, and `websiteUri` present does not mean a real website exists** — both directions burn a call. A Places-only verdict must never reach the triage queue (it is a candidate, a distinct DB state); a URL must be classified, never filtered on presence — `business.site`, Facebook, Linktree, and parked domains all arrive with a `websiteUri`. Avoid: enforce the candidate-vs-lead state split with a DB constraint, not a convention, and build the six-way verdict (with `unverifiable`) from receipts only.
4. **Storing Google Places content as if it were yours.** The obvious wide `leads` table with all Places fields on it is precisely the "business listings database" section 3.2.3 prohibits, and it is the hardest schema to unwind later. Avoid: two tables from the first migration — a TTL'd `places_cache` keyed by `place_id`, and a `leads` table whose durable fields come from Overture/Comptroller/your own probes, enforced by a provenance foreign-key constraint.
5. **RLS bugs are invisible in a single-org-for-now multi-tenant schema** — every policy is unexercised because there is nothing to leak to, until a real second tenant discovers them. Avoid: seed a second org in the test database from day one; every RLS test asserts cross-org denial through the user client (never `serviceDb`, which is blind to grants), watched failing first, SQLSTATE pinned.

### Competitive Landscape (full detail in COMPETITORS.md)

20+ products compete on "no website filter" alone at a price floor near $0 (free Chrome extensions, n8n workflows). The agency-tier sweet spot is $25 to $120/month, and Siteless's own input costs (roughly $21 to $37/mo) fit comfortably under a $49/mo positioning against that band. Webleadr is the one competitor worth studying directly (buy it as competitive intelligence): mobile-first PWA, three-bucket classification, an AI-vision design score — but no receipts, no FP tracking, no scheduling, no Spanish. The starkest evidence in the whole survey is an Apify actor with 993 users, 100%-successful runs, and a 1.88-star rating — proof that naive no-website detection, delivered exactly as advertised, still fails users because nobody verified it. Zero surveyed competitors serve the RGV's bilingual market (Spanish trade vocabulary, es-MX Facebook pages) — a real, defensible gap for a locally-built product. Anti-patterns confirmed by the survey and already excluded from PROJECT.md: daily-search quotas as the pricing lever, bring-your-own-API-key pricing, a-la-carte per-endpoint billing, bundled outreach sending, vanity audit PDFs (structurally void for a no-website business), and lifetime-deal/OTO ladders.

### Data Sources & Legality (full detail in DATA-SOURCES.md)

Empirically measured and licence-checked: Overture Maps (CDLA-Permissive-2.0/Apache-2.0/CC0, all commercial-safe, no share-alike) is the best single enumerator at 57,494 RGV places, refreshed monthly. TX Comptroller Active Sales Tax Permit Holders (`jrea-zgmq`, Public Domain, refreshed daily-ish) reaches 34,928 RGV outlets including 38% sole proprietors, but structurally under-counts pure-service businesses that sell no taxable product (only 767 personal-care outlets RGV-wide) — pair it with Overture, which has the opposite bias. OSM/Overpass, Yelp Fusion, Meta Graph API, SerpApi, and Apify's Google Maps actors are all skips for v1, each for a distinct, specific reason documented in the cross-cutting findings above — not a blanket "too expensive" judgment. Domain-liveness probing (DNS, then parking-nameserver match, then HTTP redirect chain, then final-host classification, then TLS, then content heuristics, in that cost order) is free, self-hosted, and is the layer that actually produces receipts; DNS-based parked-domain detection alone is reported around 92.8% accurate without fetching a page. County DBA/assumed-name filings are a documented skip for v1 automation (three portals with no API, one county — Willacy — with no online index at all) in favor of a manual lookup link on the lead detail screen.

## Implications for Roadmap

Every research file that discusses build order (ARCHITECTURE.md and PITFALLS.md explicitly, the others by dependency) converges on the same shape. This should be the roadmap's starting skeleton, not just an input to it.

### Phase 0: Foundations
**Rationale:** Everything else depends on it, and — uniquely for this project — the schema decisions made here prevent nine of seventeen catalogued pitfalls that are expensive cross-cutting migrations if deferred (candidate-vs-lead state separation, the six-value verdict enum, two-name-field provenance, `place_id`-vs-lead-UUID separation, the ephemeral/durable table split, `org_id NOT NULL`, the tile grid, `suppressions`/`do_not_contact`, `timestamptz` plus `APP_TZ`). Resist the urge to make this phase small.
**Delivers:** Next.js + Clerk (v2 claims) + new standalone Supabase project; `orgs`/`users`/`memberships`; `app.jwt()`/`app.current_org_id()` RLS helpers; a second seeded test org with cross-org-denial tests (watched red first); `withRollback`/`actAs` test harness ported from BIS; CI (`verify` + `e2e`).
**Addresses:** org-scoped auth requirement; multi-tenant-ready schema requirement.
**Avoids:** Pitfall 13 (invisible RLS bugs in a single-org schema) — needs `/gsd-secure-phase`.

### Phase 1: Budget Ledger (parallel with Phase 2)
**Rationale:** Nothing may spend money before this exists — shipping the Places adapter with a "we'll add the cap later" TODO is how a nightly crawl produces a surprise invoice.
**Delivers:** `budget_periods`/`cost_reservations`/`cost_ledger` (integer micro-USD), the reserve-then-settle Postgres function (race-free under concurrent workers, verified against READ COMMITTED semantics), a `skuForFieldMask()` pure function with a unit test per Google field, and an independent per-API daily quota cap set in the Google Cloud console (Google budget alerts do not stop spending — this is documented, verified fact).
**Uses:** Postgres conditional `UPDATE ... RETURNING`, no advisory lock.
**Research flag:** No — the pattern is verified; the concurrency test is the deliverable.

### Phase 2: Free-Data Spine (parallel with Phase 1)
**Rationale:** The durable business record cannot legally be built from Places, so a place for durable name/address/phone to come from must exist before the Google adapter has anything to attach a `place_id` to (Cross-Cutting Finding 1).
**Delivers:** Overture (TX-filtered, `basic_category` not the soon-removed `categories`) and TX Comptroller sales-tax-permit bulk ingest (CI/DuckDB, offline — never in a Vercel function); normalize, block, and provisionally resolve entities into `businesses`; the provenance FK constraint enforcing durable-cites-durable; county/country geography clipping (42% of a naive bbox is Mexico).
**Research flag:** Yes — Overture's real RGV duplicate rate, junk rate, and the right `confidence` cutoff are asserted, not measured, in research; measure on ingest, don't assume.

### Phase 3: Places Adapter (transient verifier)
**Rationale:** Legal/pricing structure is settled by research; this phase turns Places from "primary source" into "authoritative but discarded verifier," per Cross-Cutting Finding 1.
**Delivers:** Text Search Enterprise field mask (allow-list, code-reviewed), `includePureServiceAreaBusinesses: true` (or the trades cluster silently loses its highest-ticket segment), tile grid with saturation detection (60-result ceiling per query), Text Search Essentials (IDs Only) for free nightly change detection, `google_place_refs` (durable, `place_id` only) plus `source_records` (ephemeral, up to 21-day TTL) split.
**Avoids:** Pitfall 1 (field mask to SKU), Pitfall 2 (nightly-full-crawl budget blowout), Pitfall 7 (silent tile truncation), Pitfall 8 (service-area businesses excluded by default), Pitfall 11 (persisting Places content).
**Research flag:** Light — ToS/pricing are settled; tile-saturation tuning against real RGV density is empirical.

### Phase 4: Verification + Receipts
**Rationale:** Receipts must exist before the classifier, and must be structurally separate from it, or the purity boundary won't survive contact with a deadline.
**Delivers:** the cheapest-first probe ladder (free Overture signal, then DNS, then parking-NS, then HTTP redirect chain, then TLS, then content heuristics, then Firecrawl search/scrape only when free signals are inconclusive); the business.site-is-dead hard rule (a 3-line, zero-cost check shipped in the first commit); append-only `receipts` (revoke update/delete, not just a policy); a Postgres work queue (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`) sharing a transaction with the budget reservation.
**Research flag:** Yes — parking-provider nameserver signatures, Firecrawl's real per-search credit cost (docs disagree — see Gaps), and RGV-specific noise (bilingual names, shared strip-mall addresses, phone reuse across unrelated businesses) all need real-data tuning, not assumption.

### Phase 5: Classifier + Scorer (pure, versioned)
**Rationale:** Both must be pure functions with zero I/O and zero `db/` imports (enforce with an ESLint rule, not a convention) so a classifier version bump is a free replay over stored receipts, never a re-crawl.
**Delivers:** the six-way verdict rule ladder (ordered, first-match-wins, `rule_key` stored per verdict) including the `unverifiable` class; the glass-box scorer with persisted per-component breakdown; a replay CLI that reruns any classifier version against every stored `evaluation_labels` receipt set.
**Avoids:** Pitfall 4 (websiteUri-present false negatives), Pitfall 5 (bot walls collapsing into false positives), Pitfall 17 (stale verdicts, review-count gaming — log-scale-cap the review contribution).
**Research flag:** Yes — rule thresholds are the product; budget this as a tuning loop against real RGV verdicts, not a one-shot build.

### Gate: Hand-Triggered Vertical Slice (one city x one industry)
**Rationale:** Cross-Cutting Finding 8 — a nightly crawl that produces confident nonsense is worse than no crawl. Run Phases 1 through 5 end-to-end by hand, on one narrow slice, and eyeball roughly 50 verdicts before building the scheduler that will run this unattended every night.
**Delivers:** a go/no-go on verification quality, and the first real numbers for the budget model (the query-fan-out multiplier and the actual free-tier headroom are both currently estimates — see Gaps).

### Phase 6: Scheduler, Triage PWA, CRM Outbox (parallelizable once the slice is trustworthy)
**Rationale:** All three depend on a trustworthy pipeline (Phase 5) but not on each other — they can be built in parallel.
**Delivers (scheduler):** idempotent cron tick keyed on `(search_id, local_date in America/Chicago)`, a DST-safe fixed UTC hour, watermarks instead of wall-clock windows for "new since last run."
**Delivers (triage PWA):** offline-tolerant swipe triage, client-generated idempotency keys per action, `tel:` tap-to-call with E.164 normalization, receipts one tap away, verdict age shown on every card. Needs `/gsd-ui-phase` — this is the daily-use surface and the design risk is real (installed-PWA auth on a real iPhone must be tested on-device, not in a simulator).
**Delivers (CRM outbox):** transactional outbox (never an inline HTTP call from the accept transaction), raw-body HMAC-signed webhook to BIS with a deterministic idempotency key (`siteless:{lead_id}`), a tag allowlist plus a sentinel test scanning every outbound payload for internal labels (the exact `accounts.name` bug class that cost BIS three occurrences). The BIS-side unique index and endpoint are a cross-repo dependency — specify the contract now, in parallel, so this phase doesn't start blocked.
**Avoids:** Pitfall 9 (budget-cap races and duplicate cron runs), Pitfall 12 (timezone/DST bugs), Pitfall 14 (PWA cache/offline traps), Pitfall 15 (CRM duplicate pushes and webhook auth mistakes). Needs `/gsd-secure-phase` on the CRM outbox.
**Research flag:** Scheduler — no (cron semantics are settled). Triage PWA — yes, via `/gsd-ui-phase`. CRM outbox — light (pattern is settled; the BIS contract needs agreement).

### Phase 7: False-Positive Feedback Loop + Dashboard
**Rationale:** Needs human labels from Phase 6's triage screen to exist; falls out naturally once the architecture above is in place.
**Delivers:** the "actually has a site" control feeding `evaluation_labels`; measured FP rate as a first-class dashboard metric from lead #1 (not a later phase); cost-per-verified-lead; a per-verdict-class accuracy table; the classifier-replay promotion workflow (propose v2, replay at $0 over stored receipts, promote only if FP rate improves with no regression).
**Research flag:** No — falls out of the Phase 4/5 architecture if that architecture was built correctly.

### Phase Ordering Rationale

- The dependency graph, not preference, drives this order: the free-data spine must exist before Places is called (legal reason), receipts must exist before the classifier (purity reason), and the classifier must be trustworthy before the scheduler automates it (quality reason).
- Budget ledger and free-data spine are the only two phases safe to parallelize before Phase 3, because neither depends on the other.
- Scheduler, triage PWA, and CRM outbox are safe to parallelize after the vertical-slice gate, because all three consume the same trustworthy pipeline output but do not depend on each other's internals — except that the CRM outbox's idempotency-key format should be agreed during the triage PWA phase, since both live in the same offline-mutation contract.
- The manual vertical-slice gate is not optional scope — it is the difference between shipping a nightly crawl that produces confident nonsense (worse than no crawl) and one that has been eyeballed against roughly 50 real verdicts first.

### Gaps to Address

- **Steady-state monthly cost — the six files disagree on the exact number.** STACK.md models roughly $36.50/mo (full monthly Places sweep plus Firecrawl plus Haiku classification, 3x query overlap assumed); PITFALLS.md's arithmetic implies about $31 available for Places after Firecrawl Hobby, supporting roughly one full sweep per month; ARCHITECTURE.md's per-business unit cost implies 2,300 to 3,400 fully-verified businesses per month are affordable; DATA-SOURCES.md's most granular model (weekly Enterprise plus nightly free IDs-only plus Firecrawl Hobby) lands at $20.73 to $23.73/month; COMPETITORS.md's own derived estimate of a naive full sweep is about $91/month (explicitly the rejected shape). Resolve by building the committed cost-model test against the real cell list in Phase 1, before the scheduler is designed — the model's biggest single uncertainty is the query-fan-out/tile-overlap multiplier (assumed roughly 3x in one model, unmeasured elsewhere).
- **Firecrawl's per-search credit cost is internally disputed.** DATA-SOURCES.md and STACK.md both cite the official docs at 2 credits per 10 results; ARCHITECTURE.md flags that Firecrawl's own documentation states both 2 and 10 credits per 10 results in different places, depending on retention mode. Measure the actual number against the first real invoice and put the measured figure in `source_price_book` before trusting any budget model that includes it.
- **Whether each Places `pageToken` page is separately billed** — not stated explicitly in Google's docs; every SKU's per-request framing implies yes, and every cost model above assumes yes. Verify against the first real billing cycle.
- **The section 3.2.3(d)(iii) "listings or directory service" question, and whether a derived verdict counts as Content under section 3.2.3(c)** — this is a genuine legal grey area, not a research gap that more searching resolves. Get a 30-minute lawyer read before the first production Places call; the architecture is deliberately built so a "no" answer costs a config change (swap Places for the Overture-plus-probe-only path), not a rewrite.
- **Overture's real RGV duplicate rate, junk rate, and the correct `confidence` cutoff** — Overture's own documentation warns of "duplicates, a high junk rate, and low property completeness"; this research measured RGV counts but not RGV duplicate/junk rates. Measure during Phase 2 ingestion, not before.
- **The Clerk plus Next.js 16 `proxy.ts` integration pattern** — Clerk's dedicated Next-16 upgrade page 404s as of this research; the recommended pattern is assembled from Clerk's general `clerkMiddleware` reference plus Next's own upgrade docs. Re-verify against Clerk's live quickstart at the start of Phase 0.
- **RGV bilingual/Spanish-language verification coverage** — confidently identified as a market-wide gap (no competitor covers it) and a real recall problem, but there is no existing tuning data; the verification ladder's phrase lists, stopword lists, and social-detection patterns will need real RGV data to validate, not just the seed list in this research.
- **RGV agency willingness-to-pay** — no local pricing data exists for this specific market; the $25 to $120/mo "agency sweet spot" is inferred from the broader competitive survey, not from an RGV conversation. Low-stakes for the roadmap (v1 has one customer — danlo/BIS) but relevant before any external pricing decision.
- **Willacy County has no discoverable online DBA/assumed-name portal** — confirmed by direct probe (DNS failure), not just undocumented. Already scoped as a manual-lookup-only source for v1; no action needed unless Willacy coverage becomes a priority.
